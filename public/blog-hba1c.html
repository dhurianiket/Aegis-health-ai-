Blood Sugar Guide

What Does Your HbA1c Report Mean?

By Aniket Dhuri • Published May 27, 2026 • 6 Min Read

When you receive your blood test reports, seeing a test called **HbA1c** with a percentage like 5.8% or 6.5% can often feel confusing. While everyday finger-prick tests show your blood sugar at that exact moment, the HbA1c test gives you a much broader, long-term picture.

In simple words, **HbA1c shows your average blood sugar over the last 2 to 3 months (about 90 days).**

Here is how it works: inside your blood are red blood cells, which carry oxygen. Sugar in your blood naturally attaches to a protein in these cells called hemoglobin. Since red blood cells live for about 3 months, measuring this sugar attachment gives a highly reliable average of your blood sugar over that time. It is a wonderful tool for long-term blood sugar tracking because it cannot be affected by what you ate the night before the test.

Understanding the HbA1c Ranges

HbA1c Level (%) Category What This Means in Simple Words
Below 5.7% Normal Your average blood sugar over the last 3 months is in a healthy range.
5.7% to 6.4% Prediabetes Your sugar levels are slightly higher than normal. This is an early, friendly warning sign. It means you can make simple changes to your daily habits to help bring your levels back to the normal range.
6.5% and Above Diabetes Your average blood sugar is high. If you see this, it is important to work closely with a doctor to create a gentle plan to manage your health and keep your body feeling its best.

Key Things to Know About Each Range

Remember, these numbers are not a test of character or a reason to feel bad. They are simply information to help you and your family make healthy choices together:

  • Normal (Below 5.7%): This indicates things are on track. To maintain this, enjoy home-cooked balanced meals and keep moving daily.
  • Prediabetes (5.7% to 6.4%): This is a very common stage. Many Indian families have successfully managed or reversed prediabetes by doing simple things like replacing some white rice and maida with whole grains like ragi, jowar, or oats, and taking a short, pleasant walk after meals.
  • Diabetes (6.5% and Above): If your result is in this range, it indicates a need for regular care. With a combination of doctor-guided support, simple nutrition adjustments, and physical activity, you can safely manage sugar levels and lead a long, active life.

🇮🇳 An Important Note for Indian Families: HbA1c results should always be discussed with a doctor. In India, conditions like iron deficiency anemia (which is very common, especially among women) or certain inherited blood traits can sometimes make HbA1c results appear falsely high or low. A doctor will look at your overall health, not just one number.

📝 Simple Questions to Ask Your Doctor

When you consult with your doctor to review your HbA1c report, you can use these simple questions to guide your conversation:

  1. "Based on my family history, nutrition, and daily routine, what is a realistic HbA1c goal for me over the next 3 months?"
  2. "Could we try making simple lifestyle adjustments—like adding daily walks and changing some of my dietary habits—first before discussing medicine?"
  3. "Are there specific traditional local grains or food pairings you recommend to help keep my blood sugar stable?"
  4. "Are there other factors like my blood count or anemia that could be affecting my HbA1c percentage?"
  5. "When should I get my next HbA1c test done to check on our progress?"
